    AI Overview
    The principle stating that any rule or measure, when used as a target for behavior modification, will be manipulated or gamed is known as
    Goodhart's Law. 
    Formulated by British economist Charles Goodhart in 1975, the law is commonly stated as: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure". 
    Key Aspects of Goodhart's Law in Gamification: 
    • Behavioral Gaming: It describes the tendency for people to manipulate a system or metrics (like points, badges, or leaderboards) to receive rewards, rather than to improve the underlying behavior.
    • Systemic Failure: When rules are created to drive specific outcomes (e.g., "make 100 calls to get a bonus"), individuals may prioritize the metric over the quality or goal of the work.
    • Context: It is widely applied to understand how gamification initiatives can fail if they focus solely on superficial mechanics rather than meaningful engagement.
